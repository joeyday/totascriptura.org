## Bible vs. Book of Mormon

Interesting difference between the epistemology of the Bible vs. the epistemology of the Book of Mormon.

* The Bible says whatever is from God will confess that Jesus has come in the flesh (1Jn 4:1–6).
* The Book of Mormon says that whatever is from God will entice you to do good works (Moro 7:13).

## All references from James N. Anderson’s <cite>A Biblical Epistemology</cite>

[*A Biblical Epistemology? ‹ Anological Thoughts* by James N. Anderson](http://www.proginosko.com/2009/04/a-biblical-epistemology/)

God is a knower and God’s knowledge includes ‘propositional’ knowledge
: Ge 3:5; Lk 16:15; 1Co 3:20

God created all other knowers
: Ge 1:1; Rev 4:11

We can obtain knowledge of God
: Jer 31:34; Jn 14:7

In fact, everyone has knowledge of God in a limited sense, even though that knowledge may be suppressed
: Ro 1:18-23

Knowledge of God is one of the most important goals of human life
: Jer 31:34; Jn 17:3; Ac 17:24


What a person can know about God, and about other closely related matters, depends in large measure on their spiritual state
: Ps 82:5; Lk 10:21; Ro 1:21; 1Co 2:11-16; Eph 4:17-18

Our knowledge of God is necessarily limited
: Job 36:26; Isa 55:8-9; Ro 11:33

God’s Word is a higher epistemic authority than human reason or intuition
: Pr 3:5; Isa 8:20; Jer 23:29; Jn 10:35; 1Co 1:25; 2Ti 3:16-17; 1Pe 1:23-25

One can know with a high degree of confidence that the message of the gospel is true
: Lk 1:4; Ac 2:36; 17:31

People of all levels of intellectual sophistication can have a saving knowledge of God and Jesus Christ
: Dt 29:29; Lk 10:21; 1Co 1:26-28

There are objective truths: truths that are independent of human opinions and feelings
: Ex 20:16; Pr 12:17; Jn 8:44; 17:17; 19:35; 2Co 10:5; Gal 2:14

There are culture-transcending truths that can be known by people from all cultures; not all knowledge is culture-relative
: Eze 38:23; 39:7; Jn 17:23; Ac 11:20; 19:10; 20:21; Ro 15:8-13; 16:26

There is a significant ethical dimension to human belief and knowledge
: Pr 1:7; Mt 22:36-38; Ro 1:18-32; 1Co 10:31

One can be morally culpable for failing to hold certain beliefs
: Jn 16:9; 2Th 2:12; Heb 3:19; 1Jn 5:10

It is possible to know some truths via sense experience
: Ge 46:30; Lk 21:20, 30; Jn 3:11; 19:35

It is possible to know some truths apart from sense experience
: Mt 16:16-17; Lk 5:22

It is possible to know some truths by direct divine revelation, apart from natural means
: Ge 46:2-4; Eze 1:1; Da 7:1; Mt 16:16-17; Rev 1:9-11

It is possible to know moral truths
: Isa 51:7; Ro 7:1

It is possible to know future contingents
: Mt 20:17-19; Mk 13:22-23; Lk 22:34; Jn 6:64

Inductive knowledge based on sense experience is commonplace
: Mt 16:1-3; 24:32

Human testimony is an important source of knowledge
: Nu 35:30; Lk 1:2; Jn 21:24

At least some human knowledge is not dependent on human embodiment
: Lk 16:25; 23:42-43; Rev 6:9-11

Arguments *a fortiori* are a good form of reasoning
: Lk 11:13; 12:24; Ro 11:12

There is substantial continuity between divine reasoning and human reasoning
: Isa 1:18

It is possible and worthwhile for believers to reason with unbelievers about God, Jesus Christ, etc.
: Ac 17:2, 16-34; 18:4, 19; 19:8-9

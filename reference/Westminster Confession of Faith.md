Scripture prooftexts from the Westminster Confession of Faith—a superset of the Westminster Assembly’s own proofs (1647), those from the 1788 American revision, and from the OPC and PCA editions.

## Chapter 1. Of the Holy Scripture

1.1 Creation and providence show God but cannot save, so he revealed himself and put it in writing
: Ps 19:1–4; Pr 22:19–21; Isa 8:19–20; Mt 4:4, 7, 10; Lk 1:3–4; Jn 17:3; 20:31; Ro 1:19–20; 1:32–2:1, 14–15; 15:4; 1Co 1:21; 2:13–14; 10:11; 14:37; 2Ti 3:15; Heb 1:1–2; 2:2–4; 2Pe 1:19; 1Jn 5:13

1.2 The canonical list of Old and New Testament books, all inspired
: Lk 16:29, 31; 24:27, 44; Jn 5:46–47; Eph 2:20; 2Ti 3:15–16; Rev 22:18–19

1.3 The Apocrypha is uninspired and carries no authority
: Lk 24:27, 44; Ro 3:2; 2Pe 1:21; Rev 22:18–19

1.4 Scripture’s authority rests on God its author, not on any man or church
: 1Th 2:13; 2Ti 3:16; 2Pe 1:19–21; 1Jn 5:9; Rev 1:1–2

1.5 The church’s witness points to Scripture, but certainty comes from the Spirit’s inward witness
: Ps 19:7–11; Isa 55:11; 59:21; Jn 10:35; 16:13–14; Ro 11:36; 1Co 2:4–5, 9–12; 1Th 1:5; 1Ti 3:15; 2Ti 3:15; Heb 4:12; 1Jn 2:20, 27

1.6 Scripture is sufficient: nothing to be added, though the Spirit’s illumination is needed and some details of worship are left to prudence
: Jn 6:45; 1Co 2:9–12, 14–15; 11:13–14; 14:26, 40; 2Co 4:6; Gal 1:8–9; Eph 1:18; 2Th 2:2; 2Ti 3:15–17

1.7 Not everything in Scripture is equally clear, but what is necessary for salvation is plain enough for anyone
: Dt 29:29; 30:10–14; Ps 119:105, 130; Ac 17:11; 2Pe 3:16

1.8 The Hebrew and Greek texts are authoritative, and Scripture should be translated and read in every language
: Ex 20:4–6; Ps 119:89; Isa 8:20; Mt 5:18; 15:3, 6–9; 28:19–20; Mk 15:34; Lk 16:31; Jn 5:39, 46; Ac 15:15; 17:11; Ro 15:4; 1Co 14:6, 9, 11–12, 24, 27–28; Col 3:16; 2Ti 3:14–15; Rev 1:3

1.9 Scripture interprets Scripture
: Jn 5:46; Ac 15:15–16; 2Pe 1:20–21

1.10 The Holy Spirit speaking in Scripture is the final judge of all controversies
: Mt 22:29, 31; Ac 28:25; Eph 2:20; 1Jn 4:1–6


## Chapter 2. Of God, and of the Holy Trinity

2.1 The one living and true God and his attributes
: Ge 17:1; Ex 3:14; 34:6–7; Dt 4:15–16; 6:4; 1Ki 8:27; Ne 9:32–33; Job 11:7–9; 26:14; Ps 5:5–6; 11:5; 90:2; 115:3; 139:6; 145:3; Pr 16:4; Isa 6:3; 14:24; 45:5–6; Jer 10:10; 23:23–24; Na 1:2–3, 6; Mal 3:6; Lk 24:39; Jn 1:18; 3:16; 4:24; Ac 14:11, 15; Ro 1:18; 11:34, 36; 16:27; 1Co 8:4, 6; Gal 3:20; Eph 1:11; 1Th 1:9; 1Ti 1:17; Heb 10:28–31; 11:6; Jas 1:17; 1Jn 4:8, 16; Rev 4:8, 11

2.2 God’s self-sufficiency, sovereignty, perfect knowledge, and holiness, and the worship due to him
: Job 22:2–3; Ps 119:68; 145:17; 147:5; Jer 10:10; Eze 11:5; Da 4:25, 35; Lk 17:10; Jn 5:26; Ac 7:2; 15:18; 17:24–25; Ro 7:12; 9:5; 11:33–34, 36; 1Ti 6:15; Heb 4:13; Rev 4:11; 5:12–14

2.3 One God in three persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit
: Mt 3:16–17; 28:19; Jn 1:14, 18; 15:26; 2Co 13:14; Gal 4:6; Eph 2:18; Col 1:15; Heb 1:2–3; 1Jn 5:7


## Chapter 3. Of God’s Eternal Decree

3.1 God’s eternal, free, and unchangeable decree, without making him the author of sin or removing creaturely freedom
: Ps 5:4; 33:11; Pr 16:33; Hab 1:13; Mt 17:12; Jn 19:11; Ac 2:23; 4:27–28; Ro 9:15, 18; 11:33; Eph 1:11; Heb 6:17; Jas 1:13–14, 17; 1Jn 1:5

3.2 God’s decree does not depend on foreseen conditions
: 1Sa 23:11–12; Mt 11:21, 23; Ac 15:18; Ro 9:11, 13, 16, 18

3.3 Some are predestined to life and others foreordained to death, for God’s glory
: Pr 16:4; Mt 25:31, 41; Ro 9:22–23; Eph 1:5–6; 1Ti 5:21; Jude 1:6

3.4 The number of the predestined is fixed and definite
: Jn 10:14–16, 27–28; 13:18; 17:2, 6, 9–12; 2Ti 2:19

3.5 The elect were chosen in Christ before the foundation of the world, apart from foreseen faith or works
: Ro 8:28–30; 9:11, 13, 15–16; Eph 1:4–6, 9, 11–12; 2:8–9; 1Th 5:9; 2Ti 1:9

3.6 God ordained the means as well as the end: the elect are redeemed, called, justified, adopted, sanctified, and kept, and only they
: Jn 6:64–65; 8:47; 10:14–15, 26; 17:9; Ro 8:28–39; Eph 1:4–5; 2:10; 1Th 5:9–10; 2Th 2:13; Tit 2:13–14; 1Pe 1:2, 5; 1Jn 2:19

3.7 God passes by the rest and ordains them to dishonor and wrath for their sin
: Mt 11:25–26; Ro 9:17–18, 21–22; 2Ti 2:19–20; 1Pe 2:8; Jude 1:4

3.8 Predestination is to be handled with care, and is meant to produce praise, humility, and comfort
: Dt 29:29; Lk 10:20; Ro 8:33; 9:20; 11:5–6, 20, 33; Eph 1:6; 1Th 1:4–5; 2Pe 1:10


## Chapter 4. Of Creation

4.1 God created the world out of nothing in six days, and it was very good
: Ge 1; Ex 20:11; Job 26:13; 33:4; Ps 33:5–6; 104:24; Jer 10:12; Jn 1:2–3; Ac 17:24; Ro 1:20; 11:36; 1Co 8:6; Col 1:16; Heb 1:2; 11:3

4.2 Man was created male and female in God’s image, with the law written on the heart and a probationary command
: Ge 1:26–30; 2:7; 2:15–3:24; Ps 8:6–8; Ecc 7:29; 12:7; Mt 10:28; Lk 23:43; Ro 2:14–15; Eph 4:24; Col 3:10


## Chapter 5. Of Providence

5.1 God upholds, directs, and governs every creature and event
: Ge 45:7; 2Ch 16:9; Ne 9:6; Job 38–39; 40–41; Ps 33:10–11; 94:8–11; 104:24; 135:6; 145:7, 14–17; Pr 15:3; Isa 42:9; 63:14; Eze 11:5; Da 4:34–35; Mt 6:26–32; 10:29–31; Ac 15:18; 17:25–28; Ro 9:17; Eph 1:11; 3:10; Heb 1:3

5.2 Providence works through second causes, whether necessary, free, or contingent
: Ge 8:22; Ex 21:13; Dt 19:5; 1Ki 22:28–34; Isa 10:6–7; 14:24, 27; Jer 31:35; Ac 2:23

5.3 God uses means ordinarily but is free to work without, above, or against them
: 2Ki 6:6; Job 34:20; Isa 55:10–11; Da 3:27; Hos 1:7; 2:21–22; Mt 4:4; Ac 27:24, 31, 44; Ro 4:19–21

5.4 God’s providence extends even to sin, which he orders to his own ends without being its author
: Ge 50:20; 2Sa 16:10; 24:1; 1Ki 22:22–23; 2Ki 19:28; 1Ch 10:4, 13–14; 21:1; Ps 50:21; 76:10; Isa 10:6–7, 12–15; 45:7; Jn 12:40; Ac 2:23; 4:27–28; 14:16; Ro 11:32–34; 2Th 2:11; Jas 1:13–14, 17; 1Jn 2:16

5.5 God sometimes leaves his children to temptation to humble and strengthen them
: Dt 8:2–3, 5; 2Sa 24:1, 25; 2Ch 32:25–26, 31; Ps 73; 77:1–12; Mk 14:66–72; Lk 22:31–32; Jn 21:15–19; 2Co 12:7–9

5.6 God blinds, hardens, and gives over the wicked, withdrawing his grace from them
: Ge 4:8; Ex 7:3; 8:15, 32; Dt 2:30; 29:4; 2Ki 8:12–13; Ps 81:11–12; 109:6; Isa 6:9–10; 8:14; Mt 13:12; 25:29; 26:14–16; Mk 4:11–12; Lk 22:3; Ac 13:10–11; 28:26–27; Ro 1:24, 26, 28; 11:7–8; 2Co 2:15–16; 2Th 2:10–12; 1Pe 2:7–8

5.7 God cares for his church in a special way
: Isa 43:3–5, 14; Am 9:8–9; Mt 16:18; Ro 8:28; 1Ti 4:10


## Chapter 6. Of the Fall of Man, of Sin, and of the Punishment thereof

6.1 Our first parents sinned by eating the forbidden fruit, which God permitted and purposed to order to his glory
: Ge 3:13; 50:20; 2Sa 16:10; 24:1; 1Ki 22:22–23; 2Ki 19:28; 1Ch 10:4, 13–14; 21:1; Ps 50:21; 76:10; Isa 10:6–7, 12–15; 45:7; Jn 12:40; Ac 2:23; 4:27–28; Ro 11:32–34; 2Co 11:3; 2Th 2:11; Jas 1:13–14, 17; 1Jn 2:16

6.2 The fall brought loss of original righteousness, spiritual death, and defilement of the whole person
: Ge 2:17; 3:6–8; 6:5; Ecc 7:29; Jer 17:9; Ro 3:10–19, 23; 5:12; Eph 2:1–3; Tit 1:15

6.3 Adam’s guilt was imputed and his corrupted nature passed on to all his descendants
: Ge 1:27–28; 2:16–17; 5:3; Job 14:4; 15:14; Ps 51:5; Jn 3:6; Ac 17:26; Ro 5:12, 15–19; 1Co 15:21–22, 49

6.4 Original corruption leaves us averse to good and inclined to evil, and produces actual sins
: Ge 6:5; 8:21; Mt 15:19; Ro 3:10–12; 5:6; 7:18; 8:7; Eph 2:2–3; Col 1:21; Jas 1:14–15

6.5 Corruption remains in the regenerate and is still truly sin
: Pr 20:9; Ecc 7:20; Ro 7:5, 7–8, 14, 17–18, 21–23, 25; Gal 5:17; Jas 3:2; 1Jn 1:8, 10

6.6 Every sin deserves guilt, God’s wrath, the curse, death, and all miseries
: La 3:39; Mt 25:41; Ro 2:15; 3:9, 19; 6:23; 8:20; Gal 3:10; Eph 2:3; 4:18; 2Th 1:9; 1Jn 3:4


## Chapter 7. Of God’s Covenant with Man

7.1 God relates to his creatures by covenant because of the great distance between them
: 1Sa 2:25; Job 9:32–33; 22:2–3; 35:7–8; Ps 100:2–3; 113:5–6; Isa 40:13–17; Lk 17:10; Ac 17:24–25

7.2 The covenant of works promised life to Adam and his posterity on condition of perfect obedience
: Ge 2:16–17; 3:22; Hos 6:7; Ro 5:12–20; 10:5; Gal 3:10, 12

7.3 The covenant of grace offers life in Christ through faith, with the Spirit given to the elect
: Ge 3:15; Isa 42:6; Eze 36:26–27; Mk 16:15–16; Jn 3:16; 6:37, 44–45; Ac 13:48; Ro 3:20–21; 8:3; 10:6, 9; 1Co 12:3; Gal 3:11, 21; Rev 22:17

7.4 The covenant of grace is also called a testament because of Christ’s death
: Lk 22:20; 1Co 11:25; Heb 7:22; 9:15–17

7.5 Under the law the covenant was administered through promises, types, and ordinances pointing to Christ
: Ps 32:1–2, 5; Jn 8:56; Ro 4:11; 1Co 5:7; 10:1–4; 2Co 3:6–9; Gal 3:7–9, 14; Col 2:11–12; Heb 8–9; 10; 11:13

7.6 Under the gospel it is administered through the Word and the sacraments, so there is one covenant of grace in two administrations
: Ge 15:6; Ps 32:1–2; Jer 31:33–34; Mt 28:19–20; Lk 2:32; 22:20; Ac 10:34; 15:11; Ro 3:21–23, 30; 4:3, 6–8, 16–17, 23–24; 10:6–10; 1Co 1:21; 10:3–4; 11:23–25; 2Co 3:9–11; Gal 3:8–9, 14, 16; Eph 2:15–19; Col 2:17; Heb 4:2; 12:22–28; 13:8


## Chapter 8. Of Christ the Mediator

8.1 God chose Christ as Mediator and gave him a people
: Dt 18:15; Ps 2:6; 22:30; Isa 9:5–6; 42:1; 53:10; 55:4–5; Lk 1:33; Jn 3:16; 17:6; Ac 2:29–36; 3:20, 22; 17:31; Ro 8:30; 1Co 1:30; Eph 1:4; 5:23; Col 1:13; 1Ti 2:5–6; Heb 1:2; 5:5–6; 1Pe 1:19–20

8.2 The Son became incarnate in two natures united in one person
: Mt 1:18, 20–21; 16:16; Lk 1:27, 31, 35; Jn 1:1, 14; Ro 1:3–4; 9:5; Gal 4:4; Php 2:6–7; Col 2:9; 1Ti 2:5; 3:16; Heb 2:14, 16–17; 4:15; 1Pe 3:18; 1Jn 5:20

8.3 Christ was anointed with the Spirit and called by the Father to be Mediator
: Ps 45:7; Isa 61:1; Mt 28:18; Lk 4:18; Jn 1:14; 3:34; 5:22, 27; Ac 2:36; 10:38; Col 1:19; 2:3; Heb 1:8–9; 5:4–5; 7:22, 26; 12:24

8.4 Christ willingly obeyed, suffered, died, rose, ascended, and will return to judge
: Ps 40:7–8; Mt 3:15; 5:17; 13:40–42; 26–27; Mk 15:24, 37; 16:19; Lk 22:44; 24:39, 50–51; Jn 4:34; 5:28–29; 10:18; 20:25, 27; Ac 1:11; 2:23–24, 27; 10:42; 13:29, 37; Ro 6:9; 8:34; 14:9–10; 1Co 15:3–4; Gal 4:4; Php 2:8; Heb 5:8–9; 7:25; 9:24; 10:5–10; 1Pe 3:22; 2Pe 2:4; Jude 1:6

8.5 Christ’s obedience and sacrifice satisfied God’s justice and purchased an inheritance for his people
: Da 9:24, 26; Jn 17:2; Ro 3:25–26; 5:19; 2Co 5:18; Eph 1:11, 14; 5:2; Col 1:19–20; Heb 9:12, 14–16; 10:14

8.6 Christ’s redemption benefited the elect in every age through promises, types, and sacrifices
: Ge 3:15; Ro 3:25; 1Co 10:4; Gal 4:4–5; Heb 9:15; 13:8; Rev 13:8

8.7 Christ acts according to both natures, and Scripture sometimes attributes one nature’s acts to the whole person
: Lk 1:43; Jn 3:13; 10:17–18; Ac 20:28; Ro 9:5; Heb 1:3; 9:14; 1Pe 3:18; 1Jn 3:16

8.8 Christ applies redemption to all for whom he purchased it
: Ps 110:1; Mal 4:2–3; Lk 10:19; Jn 6:37, 39; 10:15–16, 27–28; 14:26; 15:13, 15; 17:6, 17; Ro 8:9, 14, 34; 15:18–19; 1Co 15:25–26; 2Co 4:13; Eph 1:7–9; Col 2:15; Heb 12:2; 1Jn 2:1–2


## Chapter 9. Of Free Will

9.1 The will has a natural liberty that is neither forced nor determined
: Dt 30:19; Isa 7:11–12; Mt 17:12; Jn 5:40; Jas 1:13–14; 4:7

9.2 Man before the fall could will good but was changeable
: Ge 1:26, 31; 2:16–17; 3:6, 17; Ecc 7:29; Col 3:10

9.3 Fallen man has lost all ability to will spiritual good for salvation
: Jn 3:3, 5–6; 6:44, 65; 15:5; Ro 3:9–10, 12, 23; 5:5–6; 8:7–8; 1Co 2:14; Eph 2:1–5; Col 2:13; Tit 3:3–5

9.4 In conversion God frees the will to desire good, though corruption remains
: Jn 8:34, 36; Ro 6:6–7, 14, 17–19, 22; 7:14–25; Gal 5:17; Php 2:13; Col 1:13; 1Jn 1:8, 10

9.5 The will is made perfectly free to good alone only in glory
: Eph 4:13; Heb 12:23; 1Jn 3:2; Jude 1:24; Rev 21:27


## Chapter 10. Of Effectual Calling

10.1 God effectually calls the elect, and only them, by his Word and Spirit
: Dt 30:6; Ps 110:3; SS 1:4; Eze 11:19; 36:26–27; Mt 11:28; Jn 3:5; 6:37, 44–45; Ac 13:48; 16:14; 26:18; Ro 6:16–18; 8:2, 28, 30; 11:7; 1Co 2:10, 12; 2Co 3:3, 6; 4:6; Eph 1:5, 10–11, 17–19; 2:1–10; Php 1:29; 2:13; 2Th 2:13–14; 2Ti 1:9–10; Tit 3:4–7; Jas 1:18; 1Pe 1:23; 2:9; Rev 22:17

10.2 Effectual calling is of God’s free grace alone, and man is passive until quickened
: Eze 36:27; Jn 5:25; 6:37; Ro 8:7–9; 9:11; 1Co 2:14; Eph 2:4–5, 8–9; 2Ti 1:9; Tit 3:4–5; 1Jn 3:9; 5:1

10.3 Elect infants and others who cannot hear the Word are saved through the Spirit
: Ge 17:7; Lk 1:15; 18:15–16; Jn 3:3, 5, 8; 16:7–8; Ac 2:38–39; 4:12; Ro 8:9; 1Jn 5:12

10.4 The non-elect are not saved, and no one can be saved apart from Christ
: Mt 7:22; 13:14–15, 20–21; 22:14; Jn 4:22; 6:37, 64–66; 8:24, 44; 13:18; 14:6; 17:3, 12; Ac 4:12; 13:48; 28:24; Ro 10:13–17; 1Co 16:22; Gal 1:6–8; Eph 2:12–13; Heb 6:4–5; 1Jn 4:2–3; 2Jn 1:9–11


## Chapter 11. Of Justification

11.1 God justifies by pardoning and imputing Christ’s righteousness, received through faith
: Jer 23:6; Jn 1:12; 6:44–45, 65; Ac 10:43; 13:38–39; Ro 3:22–28; 4:5–8; 5:15–19; 8:30; 1Co 1:30–31; 2Co 5:19, 21; Gal 2:16; Eph 1:7; 2:7–8; Php 1:29; 3:9, 19; Tit 3:5, 7

11.2 Faith is the sole instrument of justification but is never alone
: Jn 1:12; 3:18, 36; Ro 3:28; 5:1; Gal 5:6; Jas 2:17, 22, 26

11.3 Christ fully satisfied divine justice for the justified, so justification is by free grace
: Isa 45:21; 52:13–53:12; Da 9:24, 26; Zec 9:9; Mt 3:17; Mk 10:45; Jn 3:16; Ro 3:24, 26; 5:8–10, 18–19; 8:32; 2Co 5:21; Gal 3:13; Eph 1:7; 2:7; 5:2; Php 2:6–9; 1Ti 2:5–6; Heb 1:3; 10:10, 14

11.4 The elect were decreed to be justified, yet are not justified until the Spirit applies Christ to them
: Ro 4:25; 8:29–30; Gal 2:16; 3:8; 4:4; Eph 2:3; Col 1:21–22; 1Ti 2:6; Tit 3:3–7; 1Pe 1:2, 19–20

11.5 The justified can never fall from justification but may lose God’s fatherly favor
: Ps 32:5; 51; 89:30–33; Mt 6:12; 26:75; Lk 1:20; 22:32; Jn 10:28; Ro 5:1–5; 8:30–39; 1Co 11:30, 32; Heb 10:14; 1Jn 1:7, 9; 2:1–2

11.6 Justification is the same under the Old and New Testaments
: Ro 4:6–8, 22–24; 10:6–13; Gal 3:9, 13–14; Heb 13:8


## Chapter 12. Of Adoption

12.1 Adoption: the justified are taken in as God’s children, with its privileges
: Nu 6:24–26; Ps 89:30–35; 103:13; Pr 14:26; Jer 14:9; La 3:31–32; Am 9:12; Mt 6:30, 32; Jn 1:12; Ac 15:17; Ro 5:2; 8:15–17; 2Co 6:18; Gal 4:4–6; Eph 1:5; 3:12; 4:30; Heb 1:14; 4:16; 6:12; 12:6; 1Pe 1:3–4; 5:7; Rev 3:12


## Chapter 13. Of Sanctification

13.1 Sanctification begins in regeneration and is wrought through Christ’s death and resurrection and by the Spirit
: Eze 36:22–28; Jn 17:17, 19; Ac 20:32; Ro 6:5–6, 14; 8:13–14; 1Co 6:11; 2Co 7:1; Gal 5:24; Eph 3:16–19; 5:26; Php 3:10; Col 1:10–11, 28; 4:12; 1Th 5:23–24; 2Th 2:13–14; Tit 3:4–7; Heb 12:14

13.2 Sanctification reaches the whole person but remains imperfect in this life, with a continual war against remaining sin
: Ro 7:14–25; 12:1–2; Gal 5:17; Php 3:12; 1Th 5:23; 1Pe 2:11; 1Jn 1:8–10

13.3 The regenerate part prevails by the Spirit’s strength, and the saints grow in holiness
: Ro 6:14; 7:23; 8:2; 2Co 3:18; 7:1; Eph 4:15–16; 2Pe 3:18; 1Jn 5:4


## Chapter 14. Of Saving Faith

14.1 Faith is the Spirit’s work, normally through the Word, and is strengthened by the means of grace
: Mt 28:19–20; Lk 17:5; Jn 3:5; 6:44–45, 65; Ac 2:38; 20:32; Ro 1:16–17; 4:11; 10:14, 17; 1Co 1:21; 10:16; 11:23–29; 12:3; 2Co 4:13; Eph 1:17–19; 2:8; Php 1:29; 4:6–7; Tit 1:1; 3:4–7; Heb 10:39; 1Pe 1:2; 2:2; 2Pe 1:1

14.2 Faith believes and responds to all of God’s Word, and rests on Christ for salvation
: Ezr 9:4; Ps 119:10–11, 48, 97–98, 167–168; Isa 66:2; Jn 1:12; 4:42; 14:15; Ac 15:11; 16:31; 24:14; Ro 16:26; Gal 2:20; 1Th 2:13; 1Ti 4:8; 2Ti 1:9–10; Heb 4:1; 11:13; 2Pe 1:20–21; 1Jn 5:9–10

14.3 Faith varies in strength but ultimately prevails
: Mt 6:30; 8:10; Lk 22:31–32; Ro 4:19–20; 14:1–2; Eph 6:16; Col 2:2; Heb 5:13–14; 6:11–12; 10:22; 12:2; 1Jn 5:4–5


## Chapter 15. Of Repentance unto Life

15.1 Repentance is an evangelical grace to be preached
: Zec 12:10; Mk 1:15; Lk 24:47; Ac 11:18; 20:21; 2Co 7:10

15.2 Repentance means grief for sin and turning to God in obedience
: 2Ki 23:25; Ps 51:4; 119:6, 59, 106, 128; Isa 30:22; Jer 31:18–19; Eze 18:30–31; 36:31; Joel 2:12–13; Am 5:15; Lk 1:6; 2Co 7:11; 1Th 1:9

15.3 Repentance does not earn pardon but is necessary for it
: Isa 43:25; Eze 16:61–63; 36:31–32; Hos 14:2, 4; Mk 1:4; Lk 13:3, 5; Ac 17:30–31; Ro 3:24; Eph 1:7

15.4 No sin is too small to damn nor too great to be forgiven for the repentant
: Isa 1:16–18; 55:7; Mt 12:36; Ro 5:12; 6:23; 8:1; Gal 3:10

15.5 We should repent of particular sins, not merely in general
: Ps 19:13; Mt 26:75; Lk 19:8; 1Ti 1:13, 15

15.6 Confession to God is required, and public confession is also required where others are scandalized
: Jos 7:19; Ps 32:5–6; 51; Pr 28:13; Isa 55:7; Mt 18:15–18; Lk 17:3–4; 2Co 2:7–8; Gal 6:1–2; Jas 5:16; 1Jn 1:9


## Chapter 16. Of Good Works

16.1 Good works are only what God commands in his Word
: Dt 10:12–13; 1Sa 15:21–23; Isa 29:13; Mic 6:8; Mt 15:7–9; Jn 16:2; Ro 10:2; 12:2; Col 2:16–17, 20–23; Heb 13:21; 1Pe 1:18

16.2 Good works are the fruit and evidence of living faith
: Ps 116:12–14; Mt 5:16; Jn 15:8; Ro 6:22; 2Co 9:2; Eph 2:10; Php 1:11; Col 3:15–17; 1Ti 4:12; 6:1; Tit 2:5, 9–12; Jas 2:18, 22; 1Pe 2:9, 12, 15; 2Pe 1:5–10; 1Jn 2:3, 5

16.3 The ability to do good works comes wholly from the Spirit
: Isa 64:7; Eze 36:26–27; Jn 15:4–6; Ac 26:6–7; Ro 8:4–14; 2Co 3:5; Eph 3:16; Php 2:12–13; 4:13; 2Ti 1:6; Heb 6:11–12; 2Pe 1:3, 5, 10–11; Jude 1:20–21

16.4 No one can do more than God requires
: Ne 13:22; Job 9:2–3; Lk 17:10; Ro 8:21–25; Gal 5:17

16.5 Our best works cannot merit pardon or eternal life
